# Retrospective and lessons learned

The initial feature workflow reached an independently accepted local candidate. The workflow then conducted actual independent reflections, cross-role discussion and complete-draft accuracy checks before publishing a verified retrospective.

This guide adopts its selected experiments into a reusable prompt. Their effectiveness must still be measured in later work; adoption does not prove time savings or eliminate defects.

## A. Consume and acknowledge every transition

**Observed:** completed specialist assignments were not always consumed promptly, and stale dashboard states concealed the next handoff.

**Adopted safeguard:** inspect actual statuses; read exact reports; record identity, checks, findings, last native command and resource release; record disposition and acknowledged next action. Keep current state separate from history.

**Evaluate:** inspect missed or delayed completion-to-next-task transitions and stale-state corrections. Activity labels or a watchdog alone do not establish success.

## B. Use a bounded acceptance and oracle contract

**Observed:** an error-context requirement was missing despite green checks; some interaction proof used seeded/echoed/partial evidence; test discovery and report counts needed correction.

**Adopted safeguard:** map affected clauses to consumers and distinguishing observations; calibrate one owned fixture against named false positives; retain exact mode/target/bytes/output and cleanup. Discover native prerequisites and selection, then maintain one execution owner.

**Evaluate:** classify acceptance misses, fixture-only reruns, oracle reinterpretation, environment first-attempt failures and count corrections. Keep the packet bounded.

## C. Check the displayed dashboard and preserve unknowns

**Observed:** updated source did not always mean loaded UI; day-based time and sparse labels confused the user; unavailable usage categories could resemble zero.

**Adopted safeguard:** check served identity and visible loaded state, or state the exact UI-tool blocker. Use actual minutes/hours, named task units, completion annotations, scalable layout and explicit counter availability.

**Later correction:** the burndown still advanced after all feature gates completed because later observations and the live clock extended the plot. Freeze at final accepted task completion, retain later history separately, and check the completed chart with a future clock. The correction was checked through rendering code; browser QA remained blocked by the environment.

**Evaluate:** record stale captions, task-clock extension and ambiguous-counter corrections. Do not infer productivity from dashboard usage.

## Actual retrospective process

Use the [retrospective template](templates.md):

1. Freeze a factual brief and timeline with coverage limits.
2. Collect independent reflections without sharing peers' responses.
3. Establish shared facts and conduct real cross-role questions/responses.
4. Ask participants to prioritize ideas with reasons; preserve dissent.
5. Select one to three bounded experiments with role, trigger and effectiveness checks.
6. Send the complete draft for attribution/accuracy checks and feedback.
7. Publish only within existing authority, then verify parent, title, content, tables and links by readback.

Do not fabricate attendance, votes, launch settings, dialogue or measured gains. Replacement analysts and missing responses are explicit gaps. Recommendations do not create human commitments, deadlines or recurring automations.

## Product versus evidence corrections

Keep these distinct in the record: actual source defect; test fixture defect; assertion/oracle weakness; selection/count correction; environment permission failure; dashboard presentation problem. Each has a different owner and resolving evidence.

## Sharing the lessons

The guidance above abstracts workflow lessons without publishing company-source documents, internal screenshots, participant identities or private evidence links. Keep the underlying records in their approved environment and review new examples before external publication.
